# Yidong Yang

Computational social scientist at the University of Chicago, working on international
security and political economy. Most of what I do is assembling country-year and
dyad-year panels out of scattered sources, then being careful about what they can and
cannot identify.

MA student in Computational Social Science (political science concentration), expected
2027, following an MA in the Social Sciences in 2026.

### Current work

- **Strategic Symmetry** — do states that buy weapons from the same suppliers fight each
  other less? A global dyad-year panel, 1990–2014, built from SIPRI arms transfers, COW
  militarized disputes, alliances, trade and UN voting, with identification from a
  Bartik-style instrument on supplier-side shocks. With Paul Poast and Zhengyi Zhang.

- **Rivalry, Capacity, and GEO Activity** — does geopolitical rivalry push states into
  geostationary orbit, or only accelerate the ones that already could? Staggered
  difference-in-differences on 176 countries, 1963–2024. The answer is the second one.
  → [`space-geo-rivalry`](https://github.com/yidongyangcn-creator/space-geo-rivalry)
  *(APSA 2026)*

- **Sanctions and Refugee Outflows** — event-study estimates across 158 target states,
  1962–2016, using Sun–Abraham and Borusyak et al. imputation to handle staggered,
  non-absorbing treatment. With Xulan Jiang. *(APSA 2026)*

- **Populism, Democracy, and International Organization** — whether right-wing populist
  governments disengage from IOs, and whether democratic quality constrains that.
  Evidence from UN peacekeeping contributions, 1990–2020. With Xulan Jiang. *(MPSA 2026)*

### Methods and tools

Causal inference with observational panel data: staggered difference-in-differences and
the heterogeneity-robust estimators, event-history and survival models, event studies,
shift-share instruments. R and Stata daily, Python where it helps.

I try to write analysis code that someone else can actually run, and to report the
specifications that did not work next to the ones that did.
